HOLT, MI·2006–2008

Three state titles
before a driver's license.

My father trained me like a Russian Olympic coach. Before school. After school. On Sundays, when the other dads slept in.

I won three state championships in Michigan. I lost the national final to the man who went on to win four NCAAs.

I was fourteen the first time I smoked a joint.

This is not a broken-home story. Two parents who loved me. Two brothers who still do. I did everything I was supposed to do, and I still ended up on the bathroom floor of a gas station in Florida.

07 · MOMENT TWO
07 / 14
02
POMPANO BEACH, FL·TWELVE HOURS

Barefoot to Miami — thirty-five miles.

Germ had been out of prison one day. He sat on the edge of the tub while I pissed. He watched me like I was a show he'd paid to see.

I didn't look at him. I looked at the grout between the tiles and counted.

There was a silent man with a machete pacing the hallway all night. I learned his shoes. The plan, I heard them say in the kitchen, was the bathtub.

— § —

Three of them went to smoke. Two left the front door open.

No shoes. I walked until the sun came up.

— excerpt, “Held” · pompano beach, fl

08 · MOMENT THREE
08 / 14
03
ALEXANDER FARRELLY CJC·365 DAYS

One year inside for a crime he didn't commit.

They never questioned me. They put me in a van.

My first cellmate had killed his wife with a hammer. He told me on night three. A federal court has ruled this place unconstitutional. The ACLU has the affidavits.

A cell on the block burned for an hour before anyone came.

I counted the days on the wall with the edge of a commissary spoon. Three hundred and sixty-five of them. Then they dropped the charges and opened the gate and the year was already gone.

— excerpt, “365” · alexander farrelly cjc
